# Starter Quiz Bank — Original Puzzles (Kid Loop / "Smart Fella")

A starter bank of **33 original puzzles** for the Kid Loop, spanning every type in the style spec
(`../riddle-video-style-spec.md` §7). Draw from here to fill a round, then top up with the
**"How to generate more"** recipe at the bottom.

> **Originality & compliance (read once):** every puzzle here is **written for this project**. Do
> **not** reproduce riddles, answers, wording, or art from any source video. Keep puzzles
> **age-appropriate (~6–12)**, **solvable in seconds from one screen**, and **objective** where
> possible (easiest to keep CARU-safe). All facts below are general-knowledge and were sanity-checked;
> re-verify any fact before publish. The reveal always codes **mint ✓ = correct / coral ✗ = wrong**.

animated narrative. MC puzzles ship as `(a)`; described-visual puzzles ship as `(b)`.

**Countdown** is a *suggested* think-window; lengthen for reading-heavy or search-heavy puzzles
(spot-the-difference, count-the-things), shorten for fast-long pacing.

## Riddles

### Q1 — The two-handed timekeeper
- **Type:** Riddle · **Tier:** Warm-Up · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 7 s
- **Question:** "I have a face and two hands, but I never wave hello. I tell you when to go, but I never move my feet. What am I?"
- **Answer:** A clock.
- **Explanation:** A clock has a face and hands and tells the time — but it can't wave or walk.

### Q2 — Keys but no locks
- **Type:** Riddle · **Tier:** Brain-Bender · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 8 s
- **Question:** "I have keys but no locks, a space bar but nothing to drink, and letters but I send no mail. What am I?"
- **Answer:** A keyboard.
- **Explanation:** A keyboard has keys, a space bar, and letters — just not the everyday kind.

### Q3 — Say my whole name in one letter
- **Type:** Riddle (word) · **Tier:** Big-Brain · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 9 s
- **Question:** "I'm a five-letter word for a line of people waiting. Say just my first letter and you've said the whole word. What am I?"
- **Answer:** Queue.
- **Explanation:** "Queue" sounds exactly like the letter "Q" — the other four letters are silent.

### Q4 — Catch but never throw
- **Type:** Riddle · **Tier:** Brain-Bender · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 8 s
- **Question:** "You can catch me, but you can never throw me back. I visit most in winter. What am I?"
- **Answer:** A cold.
- **Explanation:** You "catch" a cold — you can't throw it — and colds go around most in winter.

### Q5 — Your silent copycat
- **Type:** Riddle · **Tier:** Warm-Up · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 7 s
- **Question:** "I follow you all day in the sun but disappear in the dark. I copy every move you make, yet I never say a word. What am I?"
- **Answer:** Your shadow.
- **Explanation:** Your shadow copies you in the light and vanishes when the light's gone.

## Lateral-thinking

### Q6 — Grows by shrinking
- **Type:** Lateral · **Tier:** Brain-Bender · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 8 s
- **Question:** "The more you take away from me, the bigger I get. What am I?"
- **Answer:** A hole.
- **Explanation:** The more you dig out of a hole, the bigger the hole becomes.

### Q7 — The ball that came back
- **Type:** Lateral · **Tier:** Big-Brain · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 10 s
- **Question:** "A kid kicks a ball. It travels ten feet, stops, and rolls right back to her. Nobody touched it and nothing was attached. How?"
- **Answer:** She kicked it straight up.
- **Explanation:** Kicked straight up, gravity brings the ball right back down to her.

### Q8 — Same day, not twins
- **Type:** Lateral · **Tier:** Big-Brain · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 10 s
- **Question:** "Two kids have the same mom, the same dad, and the same birthday — but they are NOT twins. How is that possible?"
- **Answer:** They're triplets (or more).
- **Explanation:** They're not twins because there's a third sibling — they're triplets.

### Q9 — The thermos that "knows"
- **Type:** Lateral (science) · **Tier:** Big-Brain · **Variant:** (b) or (c) · **Countdown:** 10 s
- **Question:** "A thermos keeps hot cocoa hot AND keeps an ice pop cold. How does it know which one to do?"
- **Answer:** It doesn't know — it just slows heat down.
- **Explanation:** Insulation slows heat from leaving or entering, so each thing keeps its own temperature — no "knowing" required.

## Sequence / Pattern (IQ)

### Q10 — Doubling up
- **Type:** Sequence (IQ) · **Tier:** Brain-Bender · **Variant:** (a) · **Countdown:** 8 s
- **Question:** "What number comes next? 2, 4, 8, 16, ___"
- **Options:** A) 20 · B) 24 · C) 32 · D) 18
- **Answer:** C) 32.
- **Explanation:** Each number doubles the one before: 16 × 2 = 32.

### Q11 — The famous add-the-two
- **Type:** Sequence (IQ) · **Tier:** Big-Brain · **Variant:** (a) · **Countdown:** 9 s
- **Question:** "What number comes next? 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ___"
- **Options:** A) 11 · B) 12 · C) 13 · D) 21
- **Answer:** C) 13.
- **Explanation:** Add the two numbers before it: 5 + 8 = 13 (the Fibonacci pattern).

### Q12 — Hidden in the calendar
- **Type:** Sequence (letters) · **Tier:** Big-Brain · **Variant:** (a) · **Countdown:** 10 s
- **Question:** "What letter comes next? J, F, M, A, M, J, ___"
- **Options:** A) J · B) A · C) S · D) O
- **Answer:** A) J.
- **Explanation:** They're the first letters of the months — after June comes July, so "J."

### Q13 — Work backwards
- **Type:** Number logic (IQ) · **Tier:** Brain-Bender · **Variant:** (a) or (b) · **Countdown:** 8 s
- **Question:** "Multiply me by 3, then subtract 2, and you get 10. What number am I?"
- **Options (if MC):** A) 3 · B) 4 · C) 5 · D) 6
- **Answer:** 4.
- **Explanation:** 3 × 4 = 12, and 12 − 2 = 10, so the number is 4.

### Q14 — Count by threes
- **Type:** Sequence (IQ) · **Tier:** Warm-Up · **Variant:** (a) · **Countdown:** 6 s
- **Question:** "What number comes next? 3, 6, 9, 12, ___"
- **Options:** A) 14 · B) 15 · C) 16 · D) 18
- **Answer:** B) 15.
- **Explanation:** The pattern adds 3 each time: 12 + 3 = 15.

### Q15 — Finish the pair
- **Type:** Analogy (IQ) · **Tier:** Warm-Up · **Variant:** (a) · **Countdown:** 6 s
- **Question:** "Bird is to Sky as Fish is to ___?"
- **Options:** A) Water · B) Net · C) Scales · D) Boat

## Spot-the-difference / Spot-the-error

### Q23 — Read it out loud
- **Type:** Spot-the-error · **Tier:** Warm-Up · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 7 s
- **Question:** On-screen card reads: **"I LOVE PARIS IN THE THE SPRINGTIME."** What's the mistake?
- **Answer:** The word "THE" is written twice.
- **Explanation:** Your brain auto-skips the repeated "the" — it's written twice in a row.
- **Art note (variant b):** reveal circles the second "THE" with a coral lasso + ✗; ✓ once spotted.

### Q24 — Two lemonade stands
- **Type:** Spot-the-difference · **Tier:** Brain-Bender · **Variant:** (b) · **Countdown:** 12 s
- **Question:** "Two lemonade stands look identical. Find the **3** differences." (Two side-by-side panels.)
- **Answer / the 3 differences (for the artist):**
  1. Banner dots: **left has 6, right has 5**.
  2. Right stand is **missing one cup** on the tray.
  3. Sun rays: **left has 8, right has 7**.
- **Explanation:** Three changes — banner dots (6 vs 5), a missing cup, and one fewer sun ray.
- **Art note:** two sticker-frame panels + 8–12px black center divider; reveal lassos all 3 in coral, ✓ when all found.

### Q25 — Break in the pattern

## How to generate more (repeatable recipe)

Use this to top up the bank while staying original, on-brand, and CARU-safe.

**1. Pick the slot.** Decide the round's **type** (spec §7) and **tier** (Warm-Up / Brain-Bender /
Big-Brain). Aim for a *varied* round — no two consecutive puzzles the same type or tier (spec §5).

**2. Choose the variant fit.**
- Objective facts / "which is right?" → **MC, variant (a)** (easiest to template + keep CARU-safe).
- Optical / spot-the-difference / count / lateral → **variant (b)** (annotated reveal).
- Story/logic flagship → **variant (c)** (reserve for spotlight-tier rounds).

**3. Draft with these prompts (write your own — never copy a source puzzle):**
- *Riddle:* name an everyday object, then describe it by 2–3 things it "has" or "does" that sound
  human/impossible ("keys but no locks…"). One clean answer only.
- *Lateral:* set up an expectation, then break it with a simple real-world twist (kicked *up*, not
  away). Avoid needing outside trivia.
- *Sequence/IQ:* pick a rule (×2, +3, add-the-two, first-letters-of-…), write 4–6 terms, blank the
  next. Distractors = off-by-one and the "obvious wrong rule."
- *Trivia (MC):* pick one **checkable** fact; write 1 right + 3 plausible wrongs of similar length.
- *Spot-the-difference:* design **3 crisp, countable** changes (counts, missing item, swapped
  color). Write them down for the artist.
- *Common-sense:* use a familiar setup with a small logic trap ("all but," "at least 28").
- *Perception/optical:* use a known illusion *concept* (same size, equal amount) with **our own**
  original art.

**4. Fill every field:** type · tier · question · options (if MC) · **answer** · one-line
explanation · suggested countdown (Warm-Up 5–7 s · Brain-Bender 7–9 s · Big-Brain 9–15 s; add time
for reading/search-heavy puzzles).

**5. QA before it enters the bank:**
- [ ] **Original** — not lifted from any source video or well-worn meme; our own wording/art.
- [ ] **One unambiguous answer**; explanation is one true line.
- [ ] **Solvable in seconds from a single screen**; ≤ ~8 words on the question card (spec §11.2).
- [ ] **Age-appropriate (~6–12)**, kind, non-shaming; no scary/unsafe content.
- [ ] **Facts re-verified** (for trivia); MC distractors are plausible and clearly wrong.
- [ ] Reveal maps to **mint ✓ / coral ✗**; a suggested countdown is set.
- [ ] Fits the **compliance** frame (no child-data asks inside the puzzle; parent-email CTA lives in
      the outro, not the puzzle).

**6. Log it** with the next Q-number, add a row to the **Index** table, and tag its best variant.
